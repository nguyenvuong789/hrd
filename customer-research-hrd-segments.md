# HRD Customer Research: Who Is the Best-Fit Renter?

> **Superseded for audience research.** This earlier draft used HRD first-party testimonials/forms. For the requested independent online research, use [customer-research-online-hrd-segments.md](customer-research-online-hrd-segments.md) instead.

Updated: 2026-08-30

## Scope and evidence

This is an initial synthesis from HRD first-party pages, not a statistically representative survey. Evidence reviewed:

- `/testimonials/`: three public renter stories linked to their original Google/Facebook sources.
- `/why-us/`: current positioning and stated renter requirements.
- `/renting-requests-form/`: the live intake fields and budget/lease/area choices.
- `/faqs/`: current-availability and lease questions.
- Existing HRD listing/navigation structure and English-first site.

External community research was attempted, but public search access did not provide a reliable, quote-level sample in this run. Do not treat the digital-nomad hypothesis as validated until interviews or tagged lead data are collected.

## Executive conclusion

### Best-supported core segment: foreign long-term renter / new arrival

**Confidence: High for the segment, Medium for exact size.**

All three published testimonials describe long-term renters. The stories repeatedly mention requirements-based search, viewing coordination, English communication, contract/move-in support and continued landlord communication. This is the clearest current customer evidence.

### Strong adjacent segment: relocating foreign professional or family

**Confidence: Medium.**

The site intake asks for move-in date, lease length, bedrooms, budget, pets, parking, pool, garden and work-from-home space. Those fields indicate a relocation use case, but the page does not provide enough submitted-lead data to quantify it.

### Plausible but unproven segment: digital nomad

**Confidence: Low to Medium.**

HRD has coworking and digital-nomad guide content, and short/medium lease options appear in the form. However, the three public testimonials are long-term renters, not explicitly digital nomads. Treat “digital nomad” as an acquisition hypothesis, not the primary persona yet.

### Lower-priority segments

- Local Vietnamese renter: low evidence and weak fit with the current English-first service promise.
- Luxury villa/holiday guest: inventory exists, but the strongest customer proof is long-term rental support rather than short-stay booking.
- Agents: likely competitors or referral partners, not the primary customer for HRD's foreign-renter product.

## Evidence extraction

### Theme 1: Requirements-based matching

**Frequency:** 3 of 3 testimonials; **Intensity:** High; **Confidence:** High.

Representative language:

- “listen to our wants and show us the properties that actually fit our needs” — Max Johnson testimonial.
- “shared my home requirements, budget and a little about me” — Kari Workman testimonial.
- “arranged all the flat viewings ... and sent all the information and pictures I needed beforehand” — Dennis Guardiola testimonial.

**Implication:** The product should sell a relevant shortlist and reduced search effort, not a raw database of contacts.

### Theme 2: Human coordination and English communication

**Frequency:** 3 of 3 testimonials; **Intensity:** High; **Confidence:** High.

Representative language:

- “negotiate with landlords or work around our busy schedules” — Max Johnson.
- “an excellent communicator in English” — Kari Workman.
- “perfectly prepared before seeing the properties” — Dennis Guardiola.

**Implication:** English clarity, scheduling and landlord communication are core value signals. A contact unlock alone may be less valuable than a contact plus useful English instructions.

### Theme 3: Support continues beyond discovery

**Frequency:** 2 of 3 testimonials; **Intensity:** High; **Confidence:** Medium/High.

Representative language:

- “Months later, Trinh still continues to work on our behalf communicating with our landlord.” — Max Johnson.
- “went above and beyond” through the move-in process — Kari Workman.

**Implication:** A low-touch paid product can offer a lightweight after-unlock support add-on, but HRD should not promise full relocation service until capacity exists.

### Theme 4: Availability is inherently volatile

**Frequency:** Explicitly stated in FAQ and homepage/footer; **Intensity:** High; **Confidence:** High.

Current FAQ language says most properties are presented by several agencies and “new availabilities come and go very quickly”. The homepage says the team will confirm current availability and lease details.

**Implication:** Sell a move-in fit and a current contact path, with a visible update date; never imply that a static listing is a guarantee.

## Segment profiles

## A. Foreign long-term renter / new arrival

**Primary job:** Find a suitable Da Nang home without wasting days navigating Vietnamese listings and landlord communication.

**Triggers:** New job, relocation, arrival in Da Nang, current lease ending, failed search through other agents.

**Top pains:**

1. Cannot tell which listings actually fit their requirements.
2. Language and scheduling friction with owners.
3. Uncertainty about contracts, deposits, utilities and maintenance.
4. Availability changes before they can view the property.

**Desired outcomes:** A short list that fits budget/date/area, clear photos and terms, a viewing arranged, and confidence before signing.

**Likely willingness to pay:** Medium to high for a successful shortlist, clear contact access or low-cost message/translation help. Lower willingness to pay for an unqualified phone-number bundle.

**Best HRD offer:** `Move-in matched shortlist` plus optional contact unlock.

**Reach:** Google area/property searches, expat groups, relocation referrals, coworking/community partners.

## B. Relocating professional or family

**Primary job:** Make a high-consequence housing decision before or soon after moving to Da Nang.

**Signals in current intake:** 2–5 bedrooms, 6–12 month/1-year leases, pets, parking, pool, garden, work-from-home space.

**Pains:** More complex requirements and higher cost of a bad choice; needs neighborhood fit and contract clarity.

**Best HRD offer:** Curated shortlist, building/area comparison, viewing coordination and contract checklist.

**Monetization:** Higher-value service or commission; less likely to value cheap contact credits alone.

## C. Digital nomad / remote worker

**Primary job:** Secure a furnished, internet-ready home for a flexible stay near beach, cafes and coworking.

**Evidence status:** Hypothesis only. HRD content mentions coworking and the form includes work-from-home space and shorter lease options, but no direct testimonial identifies a digital nomad.

**Likely pains to validate:** Internet reliability, flexible lease, deposit, quiet workspace, neighborhood walkability, ability to book before arrival.

**Best test:** One landing page and one short survey question, not a full repositioning.

## D. Agent / local rental intermediary

**Primary job:** Reduce time spent checking which properties match a client's move-in window.

**Evidence status:** Competitive hypothesis, not a confirmed customer segment.

**Strategic treatment:** Do not optimize the foreign-renter product around agents. If monetized later, create a separate availability desk with a separate interface and pricing.

## Jobs-to-be-done map

| Segment | Functional job | Emotional job | Social job | Product implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foreign long-term renter | Find and contact a fitting home | Feel safe and not lost in a new market | Look competent handling relocation | Matching + English contact context |

## What to test next (one-person research plan)

Do not build personas from assumptions. Collect at least five data points per segment.

1. Add a required optional field to the rental form: `Which best describes you?` — moving for work, family relocation, digital nomad/remote work, student, other.
2. Add `Where are you living now?` — Da Nang, elsewhere in Vietnam, overseas.
3. Ask every new lead one open question: `What is the hardest part of finding a rental in Da Nang?`
4. Interview 5 recent renters or leads for 15 minutes each. Ask what they tried first, what nearly stopped them, and what they would pay to avoid.
5. Track form-to-lead and lead-to-rental by segment for 30 days.

## Research questions for Contact Pass

- Would you pay for direct contact if the listing already showed only a broad area and price?
- Is the valuable part the phone number, exact address, English message, or confidence that the property fits your move-in date?
- How far in advance do you search: now, 30 days, 1–3 months, 3–6 months?
- Would you prefer a $3 single unlock, a $10 bundle, or a human-curated shortlist?
- What would make you request a refund: wrong number, already rented, changed price, unavailable on arrival date?

## Positioning recommendation

Primary message:

> Find a Da Nang rental that fits your move-in date, budget and area — with clear English information and a direct way to contact the owner or local representative.

Do not lead with `digital nomad` until interview and lead data confirm it. Use it as one acquisition angle under the broader category **foreign renters relocating to or staying in Da Nang**.

## Immediate product implication

The best-fit customer is not buying an anonymous contact list. They are buying reduced search friction and confidence that a rental fits their move-in window. A Contact Pass can work, but it should be framed as one part of a matched-rental experience:

`Free preview → move-in date + requirements → matched available options → contact unlock → optional English message help`
